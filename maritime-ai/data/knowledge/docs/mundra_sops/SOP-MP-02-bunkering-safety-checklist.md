# Mundra SOP MP-02 — Bunkering safety checklist

> Demo-world operating procedure authored for the Mundra Port Operations
> Portal / Sagar Drishti demo. Fictional document — not a publication of any
> real port authority.

Document: SOP MP-02 · Owner: Marine Department with HSE & Environment · Status: In force
Applies to: all bunker transfers within port limits (alongside and outer anchorage)
Related: PORT-N-07/2026 (monsoon suspension criteria), SOP MP-04 (Tier-1 oil spill response), MARPOL Annex I/VI overviews

## 1. General rules

1.1 Bunkering is carried out only by licensed bunker suppliers (licence
register: e.g. Saurashtra Bunkers LLP) with a booking accepted on the port
community interface. Marine Control is informed on VHF Ch 12 before hoses are
connected and after they are disconnected.

1.2 At the outer anchorage, bunker barge operations stand suspended whenever
sea state exceeds 5 or sustained wind exceeds 25 knots (Port Notice
PORT-N-07/2026). Marine Control's broadcast decides; a transfer in progress
when suspension is declared stops, drains and disconnects in a controlled
manner.

1.3 During declared monsoon conditions no new bunker operation starts against
a deteriorating forecast; suppliers shall not push a delivery into a closing
weather window.

## 2. Pre-transfer checklist (ship and barge jointly)

1. Bunker delivery note content agreed — grade, quantity, and a sulphur
   content compliant with MARPOL Annex VI for the fuel's intended use.
2. Mooring of barge alongside checked; fenders in place; safe access rigged.
3. Communications agreed: radio contact ship–barge–Marine Control (Ch 12),
   plus an agreed emergency stop signal tested before start.
4. Transfer plan agreed: quantity, sequence of tanks, pumping rate (start
   slow), topping-off rate, and maximum line pressure.
5. Scuppers and save-alls plugged; drip trays under manifolds; SOPEP/spill
   kit at the manifold on both craft; absorbents to hand.
6. Hose condition inspected (test date marked), properly supported, drip tray
   under each connection; couplings bolted, not wedged.
7. Fire precautions: no smoking, no naked lights, no hot work on deck; fire
   wires/extinguishers positioned per the berth or anchorage rule.
8. Manning: watch on deck at the manifold for the whole transfer on both
   ship and barge; soundings/ullages taken and repeated.
9. Weather check recorded: sea state, wind, forecast for the transfer window
   (see 1.2/1.3 limits).
10. Permit-to-work raised where required by the terminal (tanker berths
    LB-1–LB-3 follow the terminal's marine loading arm/manifold rules).

## 3. During transfer

3.1 Start at agreed slow rate; check all connections for weep before full
rate. Hourly quantity reconciliation ship vs barge. Rate reduced for
topping-off; clear stop signal before final tank tops.

3.2 Any sheen observed on the water around the vessel stops the transfer at
once — activate SOP MP-04 (Tier-1 response), inform Marine Control on Ch 12.
The demo world's incident register shows oil-sheen cases during bunkering and
hose disconnection as the typical Tier-1 trigger.

## 4. Completion

4.1 Hoses drained back before disconnection; blank flanges refitted; drip
trays emptied to a slop container, never overside. Bunker delivery note and
sample exchanged and retained (MARPOL Annex VI sample kept sealed on board).

4.2 Marine Control informed "hoses disconnected, bunkering completed"; the
barge departs under its own clearance. Quantity disputes are recorded on a
note of protest, not resolved by re-pumping without a fresh checklist.

## 5. Non-compliance

5.1 Unsafe bunkering (unattended manifold, missing spill kit, transfer in
excess weather) is stopped by the harbour patrol or terminal, recorded as an
HSE incident, and counts against the supplier's licence performance rating.
