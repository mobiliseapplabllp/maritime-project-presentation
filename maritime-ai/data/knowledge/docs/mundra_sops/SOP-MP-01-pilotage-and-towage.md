# Mundra SOP MP-01 — Pilotage and towage procedure

> Demo-world operating procedure authored for the Mundra Port Operations
> Portal / Sagar Drishti demo. Fictional document — not a publication of any
> real port authority.

Document: SOP MP-01 · Owner: Marine Department (Harbour Master) · Status: In force
Applies to: all vessel movements within Mundra port limits
Related: Port Notice PORT-N-07/2026 (monsoon), SOP MP-03 (monsoon SOP), schedule of marine charges (pilotage per movement)

## 1. Compulsory pilotage

1.1 Pilotage is compulsory within port limits for merchant vessels, covering
inward, outward and shifting movements, including SPM operations. Port craft
and small vessels exempted by the Harbour Master move under Marine Control
direction.

1.2 Movements are booked through the agent on the port community interface;
the outward pilot is booked only after the departure declaration set is
accepted (see CIRC-12/2026).

## 2. Communications

2.1 Initial call and distress watch: VHF Ch 16. Pilotage and port control
working channel: VHF Ch 12 — pilots, tugs and Marine Control coordinate the
movement on Ch 12. Pilot launch movements work Ch 14; mooring boats work
Ch 68; the survey launch works Ch 71.

2.2 Vessels maintain listening watch on Ch 12 from one hour before the
pilot-boarding time until all fast, and again from stations to pilot away.

## 3. Pilot boarding

3.1 The pilot boards at the pilot boarding ground in the vicinity of the
fairway buoy, from one of the pilot launches (Mundra P-1 to P-5). Boarding
speed, lee and ladder rigging follow SOLAS V/23 pilot-transfer arrangements;
a defective pilot ladder is a stop-the-job item and is recorded as a near
miss (the incident register carries exactly such cases).

3.2 Master–pilot exchange: passage plan for the movement, draft and UKC
(including the monsoon 20% additional UKC margin for capesize vessels bound
for WB-1/WB-2 under PORT-N-07/2026), tug rendezvous, berthing plan and
mooring pattern.

## 5. Towage

5.1 Tug fleet (demo world): Mundra Shakti and Mundra Veer (ASD, 52 T bollard
pull, FiFi-1), Samudra Tez (ASD, 45 T, oil-spill kit embarked), Kutch Sahas
(ASD, 40 T), Kutch Bal (conventional, 36 T). Tugs work VHF Ch 12.

5.2 Minimum tug allocation (Harbour Master may increase for wind, current,
draft or defects):

| Vessel | Tugs |
|---|---|
| Up to 150 m LOA | 1 tug (2 if thruster inoperative) |
| 150–230 m LOA | 2 tugs |
| Above 230 m / capesize at West Basin | 3 tugs, lead tug ≥ 45 T BP |
| Tankers at LB-1–LB-3 | 2 tugs, at least one FiFi-1 |
| SPM tanker operations | 2 tugs plus line boat, senior pilot |

5.3 A vessel with an inoperative main engine, windlass or thruster shall
declare the defect on the pre-arrival checklist; the Harbour Master sets the
additional towage and daylight/tide conditions for the movement.

5.4 Mooring boats Navinal-1 and Navinal-2 run lines at berths where the
mooring pattern needs boat assistance; gangs follow the berthing master's
line plan.

## 6. Weather and tide limits

6.1 Movements are planned to tidal windows for draft-constrained vessels.
Pilotage is suspended when sustained wind or sea conditions exceed the safe
boarding limit declared by Marine Control (see SOP MP-03 during the monsoon;
bunkering-related craft limits are in PORT-N-07/2026).

## 7. Records and charges

7.1 Every movement is logged (pilot, tugs, times, VCN) in the portal;
pilotage is charged per movement and towage per tug per movement under the
schedule of marine charges. Incidents during a movement (heavy fender
contact, mooring line parted, near miss) are reported before the pilot
leaves the ship and entered in the incident-management system.
