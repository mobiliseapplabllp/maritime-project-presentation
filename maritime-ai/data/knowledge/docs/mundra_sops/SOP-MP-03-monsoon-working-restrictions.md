# Mundra SOP MP-03 — Monsoon working restrictions SOP

> Demo-world operating procedure authored for the Mundra Port Operations
> Portal / Sagar Drishti demo. Fictional document — not a publication of any
> real port authority.

Document: SOP MP-03 · Owner: Marine Department (Harbour Master) · Status: In force
Applies to: declared South-West monsoon period (ordinarily June–September)
Related: Port Notice PORT-N-07/2026 (the operative notice this SOP implements), PORT-N-02/2023 (cyclone contingency pattern, withdrawn), SOP MP-01, SOP MP-02

## 1. Declaration and standing changes

1.1 The Harbour Master declares monsoon opening and closing by broadcast,
portal notice and agent message. On declaration, the following stand until
closure:

- UKC: capesize vessels at West Basin berths WB-1/WB-2 plan to 120% of the
  declared minimum under-keel clearance (the 20% additional UKC margin of
  PORT-N-07/2026), applied after squat and swell response.
- Bunkering: outer-anchorage bunker operations suspended at sea state > 5 or
  sustained wind > 25 knots (criteria of PORT-N-07/2026; procedure in MP-02).
- Moorings: pre-monsoon mooring standard for cape vessels raised — additional
  lines per the pilot's plan, winch brakes tested and set, doubling-up before
  forecast squalls.
- Anchorage: monsoon holding positions assigned by Marine Control override
  the routine priority matrix.

## 2. Pre-monsoon readiness (completed before declaration)

1. Joint inspection of fenders, bollards and cope lines at all berths; civil
   defects logged as work orders.
2. Storm mooring points for tugs, launches and mooring boats verified; craft
   engine trials done (fleet: 5 tugs, 5 pilot launches, 2 mooring boats).
3. Terminal severe-weather plans rehearsed: gantry cranes boom-up and
   storm-pinning drill, container stack height review for forecast winds,
   loose-gear sweep of aprons.
4. Hydrographic check of channel and berth-pocket depths by the survey launch
   (Bocha Survey-1, VHF Ch 71); declared depths republished.
5. Aids to navigation checked (a reported unlit channel buoy — e.g. buoy
   No. 7 in the demo incident register — is treated as a navigation hazard
   and promulgated at once).
6. Annual cyclone-season exercise run on the PORT-N-02/2023 pattern:
   time-bound evacuation of berths to sea, storm shutdown, and the all-clear
   survey sequence (channel sweep → berth structures → cranes → aids to
   navigation) before resumption.

## 3. Daily operating rhythm during the monsoon

3.1 Marine Control issues a daily marine forecast (wind, sea state, swell,
squall risk) to all stations on VHF Ch 12 and the portal; pilots and berthing
masters plan movements to tidal and weather windows.

3.2 Pilot boarding: suspended when conditions at the boarding ground exceed
the safe boarding limit declared by Marine Control; vessels hold at the
anchorage. Draft-constrained movements shift to daylight/slack windows where
needed.

3.3 Squall passage: cargo work on exposed cranes is paused at the terminal's
wind alarm limit; a standby tug may be ordered for a vessel ranging at her
berth; mooring watches doubled. Mooring-line partings during squalls, spring
tides or passing-vessel surge are reported and investigated (a recurring
demo-world incident pattern).

## 4. Cyclone escalation

4.1 On an IMD alert for a cyclonic system affecting the Kutch coast, the
Harbour Master shifts from this SOP to a contingency notice on the
PORT-N-02/2023 pattern: fresh berthing stops, vessels complete or secure
cargo and vacate berths to open sea or designated anchorage by a stated time,
port craft go to storm moorings, and the port closes until the post-storm
all-clear survey passes.

## 5. Records

5.1 Suspensions, resumptions, UKC waiver refusals and weather-hold decisions
are logged in the portal against the affected port calls, so the season's
waiting-time and berth-occupancy analytics correctly attribute weather
delay.
