# Mundra SOP MP-04 — Oil spill Tier-1 response SOP

> Demo-world operating procedure authored for the Mundra Port Operations
> Portal / Sagar Drishti demo. Fictional document — not a publication of any
> real port authority.

Document: SOP MP-04 · Owner: HSE & Environment (Chief — HSE) with Marine Department · Status: In force
Applies to: oil spills and sheens within Mundra port limits up to Tier-1 scale
Related: SOP MP-02 (bunkering), MARPOL Annex I overview, incident-management workflow

## 1. Tiered response concept

1.1 Following the IMO tiered-preparedness model: Tier 1 — small operational
spill at or near a facility, handled with port and operator resources (this
SOP); Tier 2 — larger or spreading spill needing regional/mutual-aid
resources; Tier 3 — major spill requiring national response. Under India's
National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan (NOS-DCP) the Indian Coast Guard
is the central coordinating agency, so any escalation beyond Tier 1 runs
through the Coast Guard.

1.2 Typical Tier-1 triggers in the demo world: oil sheen at a berth during
bunkering, hose disconnection, ballast operations or sludge transfer; minor
overside leakage from machinery spaces.

## 2. Immediate actions (first 15 minutes)

1. STOP the source: halt the transfer/operation, close valves, plug the leak
   (MP-02 makes the emergency stop signal part of every bunkering).
2. Report: first informer calls Marine Control on VHF Ch 12 (or Ch 16) —
   position/berth, source if known, estimated size and drift. Marine Control
   opens an ENVIRONMENT / OIL_SPILL incident in the portal (severity per the
   matrix; Tier-1 cases seed as MEDIUM–HIGH).
3. Restrict: Marine Control broadcasts to restrict small-craft movement near
   the affected berth; the berth may be closed to fresh movements.
4. Muster: the on-scene team (terminal HSE, ship's SOPEP team, harbour
   patrol) musters at the berth; downwind ignition sources controlled.

## 3. Containment and recovery

3.1 Boom deployment: containment boom is deployed around the sheen/slick and
the vessel's manifold area as a precaution; the skimmer is brought to
standby. Tug Samudra Tez (TUG-05) embarks the port's oil-spill kit and is
tasked for boom towing and on-water work; mooring boats assist with boom
positioning.

3.2 Recovery: skimmer and sorbents recover free product; sorbent pads/booms
used at the shoreline touch points. Recovered oil and used sorbents go to
sealed drums for disposal through the licensed waste contractor — never back
overside.

3.3 Dispersants are NOT used at Tier 1 in port waters without the explicit
direction of the authorities.

## 4. Sampling and notifications

4.1 Water samples are collected for analysis from the slick and a control
point (the portal's evidence pack pattern: water-sample analysis, site
photographs, master's statement via the agent).

4.2 Notifications logged in the communications register:
- Coast Guard Station Okha — informed with reference number (mandatory for
  any escalation; keeps NOS-DCP coordination warm).
- GPCB regional office — informed as a precaution; sampling arranged.
- Harbour Master, Chief — HSE & Environment, terminal management —
  preliminary incident notification.
- Vessel's agent — master's statement and crew list; P&I correspondent and
  surveyor attend for a ship-source spill.

## 5. Investigation, cost recovery and closure

5.1 Root cause is investigated on the portal workflow (typical demo-world
causes: worn hoses, SOP not followed at disconnection, equipment fatigue).
Corrective and preventive actions are tracked to closure — e.g. hose-handling
refresher training, halved hose replacement intervals.

5.2 Clean-up costs and damages for a ship-source spill are recoverable from
the shipowner under the civil-liability provisions of the Merchant Shipping
Act framework; the P&I club's letter of undertaking is obtained before
sailing where exposure is material.

5.3 The incident closes when recovery is complete, waste is disposed of with
manifests on file, notifications are answered, and the RCA with preventive
actions is approved. Tier-1 performance indicator: boom in the water within
30 minutes of report at any wet berth.

## 6. Escalation test

6.1 If the slick escapes the boom, threatens the intake channels or
mangroves, or exceeds the port's recovery capacity, the Harbour Master
declares Tier 2 and hands coordination to the Coast Guard under NOS-DCP,
with the port in support.
