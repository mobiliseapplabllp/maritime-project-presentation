# Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 — structure and key parts

> Reference summary compiled from public sources for the Sagar Drishti demo
> library. This is a plain-language overview in the library's own words, not
> the text of the Act; consult the official gazette text for legal use.

Portal instrument register entry — Ref No MSA-1958, type ACT, status IN FORCE,
issued by the Parliament of India (assented 30 October 1958, brought into force
from 1960). Register summary (verbatim): "Principal Indian statute governing
merchant shipping — registration, certification, safety, crew and liability."

## What the Act is

The Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 is India's principal merchant shipping
statute. It consolidates the law on ships, seafarers and shipowners, gives
effect in Indian law to the major IMO and ILO instruments India has ratified,
and is administered by the Directorate General of Shipping (DG Shipping) under
the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways. It has been amended many times
(notably to absorb SOLAS, MARPOL, STCW and MLC obligations), and detailed
requirements sit in rules made under it — for example the Merchant Shipping
(STCW) Rules, 2014.

## Structure — the key parts in plain language

- Administration: establishes the National Shipping Board and the shipping
  administration — the Director-General of Shipping, Mercantile Marine
  Departments, surveyors, shipping masters and seamen's welfare machinery.
- Registration of Indian ships: which ships must be registered in India,
  ownership qualifications, the register, tonnage measurement, name and flag,
  mortgages and transfers.
- Certificates of officers: manning scales and the requirement that masters,
  mates and engineers hold certificates of competency appropriate to the ship.
- Seamen and apprentices: engagement and discharge of crew, articles of
  agreement, wages and allotments, health and accommodation, welfare,
  disciplinary provisions and repatriation — the seafarer-protection core of
  the Act (now read with India's MLC obligations).
- Passenger ships: survey and certification of passenger ships and special
  trade passenger ships.
- Safety: construction and survey, life-saving appliances, radio, load lines,
  cargo safety (including grain and dangerous goods) — the SOLAS-implementing
  part; unseaworthy ships may be detained.
- Collisions and accidents: division of loss, duty to render assistance after
  collision, and reporting of casualties.
- Limitation of liability: the shipowner's right to limit liability for
  maritime claims in line with the international limitation regime.
- Civil liability for oil pollution: liability and compulsory insurance for
  oil pollution damage, implementing the CLC/Fund regime, alongside powers to
  deal with pollution casualties.
- Investigations and inquiries: shipping casualty investigations, courts of
  marine inquiry, and powers over certificates of erring officers.
- Wreck and salvage: receivers of wreck, dealing with wreck, and salvage.
- Control of Indian ships and coasting trade: licensing of ships (chartering
  in), coasting-trade rules for Indian-flag priority on the coast.
- Sailing vessels and fishing vessels: separate lighter-touch regimes for
  these classes.
- Penalties and procedure: offences, penalties and enforcement mechanics.

## Why it matters at a port

Port state control inspections, crew certificate checks, casualty reporting,
detention of unseaworthy or substandard ships, pollution response liability and
wreck removal at an Indian port all trace back to powers under this Act and
its rules. For Mundra demo-world workflows: PSC/FSI inspections, seafarer
document checks (CDC, INDoS, certificates of competency) and oil-spill
liability notices all cite this statute as the parent law.
