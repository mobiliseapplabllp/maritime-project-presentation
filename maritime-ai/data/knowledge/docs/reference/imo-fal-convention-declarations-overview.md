# IMO FAL Convention — declarations overview

> Reference summary compiled from public sources for the Sagar Drishti demo
> library. This is a plain-language overview in the library's own words, not
> convention text; consult the FAL Convention and its annex for legal use.

## What the FAL Convention is

The Convention on Facilitation of International Maritime Traffic (FAL), 1965,
as amended, is the IMO treaty that simplifies and standardises the paperwork
and formalities around a ship's arrival, stay and departure. Its annex lays
down Standards (binding) and Recommended Practices, built on the principle
that public authorities should not demand more data than necessary, should
accept standard forms, and should not delay the ship.

## The standard FAL declarations

The convention standardises the documents public authorities may require, the
core set being the numbered FAL forms:

1. FAL Form 1 — General Declaration: the master's basic declaration of the
   ship, voyage, arrival/departure particulars and agent.
2. FAL Form 2 — Cargo Declaration: cargo on board, to be loaded or discharged
   (in practice often satisfied by the manifest).
3. FAL Form 3 — Ship's Stores Declaration: stores, bunkers remaining on
   board, and similar consumables.
4. FAL Form 4 — Crew's Effects Declaration: dutiable or restricted personal
   effects of the crew.
5. FAL Form 5 — Crew List: the crew with ranks, nationalities and identity
   documents.
6. FAL Form 6 — Passenger List: passengers where carried.
7. FAL Form 7 — Dangerous Goods: the dangerous goods manifest/declaration
   with IMDG particulars.

Alongside these, arrival formalities commonly involve the security-related
pre-arrival information under SOLAS XI-2, the Maritime Declaration of Health
under the health regulations, and customs/immigration clearances — the FAL
principle being one submission, standard content, no duplicated demands.

## The single window (2022 amendments)

Amendments adopted in 2022 (in force from 1 January 2024) made the maritime
single window mandatory: public authorities must operate electronic exchange
of the FAL data set for arrival, stay and departure, moving clearance off
paper. The convention also encourages advance submission, 24/7 clearance for
low-risk traffic, and cooperation between authorities so the ship deals with
one interface.

## Shore leave and facilitation

The FAL regime (with the ISPS Code and MLC) supports crew shore leave and
access to shore-based welfare: security measures must be balanced against the
seafarer's need to go ashore — reflected at the demo port by the Gate 3
shore-leave route in ISPS-ADV-02/2026 rather than a denial of leave.

## Local implementation in the demo world

The Mundra demo portal's clearance workflow is a maritime single window in
miniature: Circular CIRC-12/2026 makes electronic lodgement of the FAL 1–7
data mandatory through the port community interface (superseding the interim
CIRC-03/2023 paper/email format), with submission windows before arrival and
departure and a declared-outage fallback. See that circular in this library
for the operative local rules.
