# Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 — titles overview

> Reference summary compiled from public sources for the Sagar Drishti demo
> library. This is a plain-language overview in the library's own words, not
> convention text; consult the ILO's consolidated MLC, 2006 for legal use.

## What the MLC is

The Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 (MLC, 2006) is the ILO's consolidated
"seafarers' bill of rights" — it gathers dozens of older maritime labour
instruments into one convention covering nearly every aspect of a seafarer's
working and living conditions. It entered into force in 2013; India ratified
it in 2015 and implements it through the Merchant Shipping Act framework and
rules. The convention pairs each Regulation with a mandatory Standard (Part A)
and guidance (Part B), organised under five Titles.

## The five Titles in plain language

- Title 1 — Minimum requirements for seafarers to work on a ship: minimum age
  (16, with night-work and hazardous-work protections under 18), medical
  fitness certification (a seafarer medical certificate is valid at most two
  years), training and qualifications, and regulation of recruitment and
  placement services (manning agents must be licensed and may not charge the
  seafarer for placement).
- Title 2 — Conditions of employment: a signed seafarer's employment agreement
  (SEA), wages paid at least monthly with allotments, hours of work and rest
  (maximum hours of work or minimum hours of rest limits), paid annual leave,
  repatriation at the shipowner's cost, compensation for ship loss, manning
  levels, and career development.
- Title 3 — Accommodation, recreational facilities, food and catering: cabin
  and mess standards on new ships, recreational facilities, and food — free of
  charge, of adequate quality and quantity, prepared by trained cooks.
- Title 4 — Health protection, medical care, welfare and social security
  protection: medical care on board and ashore (at no cost to the seafarer),
  shipowners' liability for sickness and injury, occupational safety and
  health, access to shore-based welfare facilities, and social security.
- Title 5 — Compliance and enforcement: flag state inspection and — for ships
  of 500 GT and over on international voyages — certification: the Maritime
  Labour Certificate plus the Declaration of Maritime Labour Compliance (DMLC)
  carried on board; port state inspections; and the on-board and onshore
  complaint procedures. The 2014 amendments added compulsory financial
  security against abandonment of seafarers and for death and disability
  claims, evidenced by certificates posted on board.

## Port relevance in the demo world

MLC appears at Mundra as: MLC-type PSC inspections in the inspection module,
checks that a vessel's Maritime Labour Certificate is valid (an expired MLC
certificate is a stop-the-call notification in the demo portal), seafarer
medical certificates limited to two years in the crewing records, licensed
manning agencies in the licence register, and shore-leave facilitation via
Gate 3 in the ISPS advisory. Crew-wage or abandonment complaints at anchor or
alongside are handled through the port state's MLC complaint channel.
