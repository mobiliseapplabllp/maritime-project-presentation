## What STCW is

The International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and
Watchkeeping for Seafarers (STCW), 1978, with its 1995 overhaul and the 2010
Manila amendments, sets the global minimum standards a seafarer must meet to
serve in a given capacity. The STCW Code's Part A holds the mandatory
competence tables; Part B is guidance. Certificates issued by one party are
recognised by others through endorsement, which is what makes crews
internationally employable.

## Structure of the convention chapters

- Chapter I — General provisions: definitions, certificate format,
  revalidation (certificates of competency are revalidated at intervals not
  exceeding five years), medical standards (medical certificates valid at most
  two years), quality systems, and fraud prevention.
- Chapter II — Master and deck department: officer in charge of a
  navigational watch, chief mate and master, with tonnage bands.
- Chapter III — Engine department: officer in charge of an engineering watch,
  second engineer and chief engineer, with power bands; electro-technical
  officer (ETO) certification came in with Manila 2010.
- Chapter IV — Radiocommunication operators: GMDSS operator certification
  (the GOC most deck officers hold).
- Chapter V — Special training: additional certification for tanker personnel
  (oil, chemical, gas), and special-ship training (e.g. passenger ships; polar
  and IGF training were added later).
- Chapter VI — Emergency, occupational safety, security and survival:
  the certificates of proficiency everyone at sea holds — Basic Safety
  Training (personal survival, fire prevention/fighting, elementary first
  aid, personal safety and social responsibility), survival craft (PSCRB),
  advanced fire fighting, medical first aid/care, and security training.
- Chapter VII — Alternative certification: functional certification pathways.
- Chapter VIII — Watchkeeping: fitness for duty and rest hours (minimum 10
  hours of rest in 24, 77 in 7 days, alignable with MLC), watch arrangements
  and principles to be observed.

## Certificate types in practice

- CoC — Certificate of Competency: the licence to serve as officer at a given
  level (e.g. Master FG; MEO Class 1/2 on the engine side in Indian usage).
- CoP — Certificate of Proficiency: task/skill certificates (BST, AFF, PSCRB,
  tanker endorsements, security awareness/designated duties).
- Endorsements: the flag's recognition attesting a CoC for service on its
  ships; watchkeeping ratings and able-seafarer grades are certified too.

## The Indian rules of 2014

The Merchant Shipping (Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping)
Rules, 2014 carry the Manila-amended convention into Indian law under the
Merchant Shipping Act, 1958. DG Shipping is the administration: it approves
maritime training institutes, conducts/oversees examinations, issues CoCs,
CoPs and endorsements, maintains the INDoS seafarer identity database, and
issues the Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC) that records sea service.
In the Mundra demo world, seafarer records show exactly this document set —
CDC number, INDoS number, CoC grade, GMDSS GOC, medical fitness (two-year
validity) and STCW safety certificates — issued by "DG Shipping, India".

## Port relevance

Crew certificate checks during port state control and flag inspections,
crewing-desk verification before sign-on, rest-hour compliance in casualty
investigations, and the currency alerts the demo portal raises when a
certificate (say a medical or BST) is expiring or expired.
