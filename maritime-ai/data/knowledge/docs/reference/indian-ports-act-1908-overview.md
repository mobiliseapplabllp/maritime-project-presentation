# Indian Ports Act, 1908 — basics

> Reference summary compiled from public sources for the Sagar Drishti demo
> library. This is a plain-language overview in the library's own words, not
> the text of the Act; consult the official gazette text for legal use.

## What the Act is

The Indian Ports Act, 1908 is the century-old framework statute for the safety
and conservancy of ports in India. Where the Major Port Authorities Act, 2021
governs who runs a major port, the 1908 Act supplies the basic port-police
powers that apply at ports generally — including the non-major ports
administered by state governments and state maritime boards. Mundra, as a
Gujarat non-major port, sits under this framework read with Gujarat state law
(the Gujarat Maritime Board regime).

## Core concepts in plain language

- Declared ports and port limits: government notifies which places are ports
  under the Act and fixes their port limits — the water and foreshore within
  which the Act's rules bite.
- Port rules: the government may make rules for a port on practically every
  operational subject — regulating vessel traffic and berthing, anchoring
  stations, speed, use of tugs and moorings, ballast and cargo handling,
  keeping fairways clear, fire precautions, and the time and manner of
  entering or leaving.
- Conservator of the port: every port has a conservator (at Mundra the
  demo-world office of the Harbour Master, with a Deputy Conservator in the
  role directory) who exercises the day-to-day powers: directing where a
  vessel berths or anchors, ordering a vessel to be moved, boarding and
  inspection, and removing obstructions.
- Safety and conservancy: prohibitions with penalties on throwing ballast or
  rubbish into port waters, damaging buoys and beacons, careless navigation,
  and unauthorised interference with moorings — the origin of the modern
  "no discharge in port waters" position.
- Port dues and fees: the legal basis for levying port dues on vessels
  entering the port, plus fees for pilotage and other services, published as a
  scale of rates.
- Accidents and wreck: masters must report accidents in the port; the
  conservator can deal with wrecks or obstructions that endanger navigation.
- Health and quarantine hooks: the Act interfaces with health law for
  pratique and quarantine at ports.

## Why it matters in the demo world

Almost every operational instrument in the Mundra demo portal is, in form, an
exercise of 1908-Act-style conservancy powers: the Harbour Master's monsoon
working restrictions (PORT-N-07/2026), the cyclone evacuation notice
(PORT-N-02/2023), anchorage allocation policy, and the schedule of marine
charges (pilotage per movement, tug hire, garbage reception fees). When a
document in this library says "issued by the Harbour Master under the port's
standing powers", this Act's framework is the pattern being followed.
