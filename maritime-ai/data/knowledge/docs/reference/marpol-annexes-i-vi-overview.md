Portal instrument register entry — Ref No MARPOL-73/78, type CONVENTION,
status IN FORCE, issued by the IMO (convention 1973, protocol 1978, in force
2 October 1983). Register summary (verbatim): "Annexes I–VI: oil, chemicals,
packaged goods, sewage, garbage and air emissions from ships."

## What MARPOL is

The International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships
(MARPOL) is the main IMO treaty on preventing operational and accidental
pollution from ships. It works through six technical annexes, each with its own
discharge standards, equipment and survey requirements, record books, and — for
several annexes — an obligation on ports to provide adequate reception
facilities for the wastes ships cannot discharge at sea.

## The annexes in plain language

- Annex I — Oil: controls discharge of oil and oily mixtures from machinery
  spaces and cargo areas of tankers; requires oil filtering equipment, the Oil
  Record Book, IOPP certificate, double hulls for tankers, and the shipboard
  oil pollution emergency plan (SOPEP). Port link: sludge and bilge landings,
  and the Tier-1 spill response SOP in this library.
- Annex II — Noxious liquid substances in bulk: categorises chemical cargoes
  (X, Y, Z) and sets stripping, prewash and discharge rules for chemical
  tanker operations, with the Cargo Record Book.
- Annex III — Harmful substances carried by sea in packaged form: packaging,
  marking, labelling and documentation rules for marine pollutants inside the
  IMDG framework.
- Annex IV — Sewage: discharge standards and holding/treatment plant
  requirements; untreated sewage stays out of nearshore waters, hence sewage
  landings or treated discharge only.
- Annex V — Garbage: the "nothing overboard by default" annex. Discharge of
  garbage at sea is generally prohibited (plastics absolutely), with narrow
  exceptions such as some food waste far from land. Requires the garbage
  management plan, placards and the Garbage Record Book, and segregation by
  category (plastics; food waste; domestic waste; cooking oil; incinerator
  ash; operational waste; e-waste; cargo residues). Port link: Mundra demo
  circular CIRC-09/2026 sets the garbage reception fees and the segregation
  certificate for landings above 2 m³.
- Annex VI — Air pollution and energy efficiency: limits SOx (0.50% global
  sulphur cap on fuel, stricter in emission control areas), NOx tiers for
  engines, bans deliberate ozone-depleting emissions, regulates shipboard
  incineration, and carries the energy-efficiency regime (EEDI/EEXI, SEEMP and
  the CII carbon-intensity rating).

## Port relevance

For a port team, MARPOL is a daily-operations document: reception of garbage,
sludge and slops; watching for sheen during bunkering and hose work; verifying
record books during PSC; and checking bunker delivery notes for sulphur
compliance. Alleged inadequacy of reception facilities is reportable to the
flag and to the IMO in a standard format.
