# SOLAS 1974 — chapters overview

> Reference summary compiled from public sources for the Sagar Drishti demo
> library. This is a plain-language overview in the library's own words, not
> convention text; consult the current consolidated SOLAS edition for legal use.

Portal instrument register entry — Ref No SOLAS-74, type CONVENTION, status IN
FORCE, issued by the IMO (adopted 1 November 1974, in force 25 May 1980).
Register summary (verbatim): "Core IMO convention on ship safety —
construction, fire protection, life-saving, radio, navigation."

## What SOLAS is

The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS), 1974, as
amended, is the central IMO treaty on ship safety. It applies broadly to ships
on international voyages, sets minimum standards for construction, equipment
and operation, and is kept current through the tacit-amendment procedure, so
its chapters evolve continuously. Flag states certify compliance; port states
verify it through PSC inspections.

## The chapters in plain language

- Chapter I — General provisions: application, surveys and the certificates a
  ship must carry (and which PSC officers check first).
- Chapter II-1 — Construction: subdivision and damage stability, machinery and
  electrical installations.
- Chapter II-2 — Fire protection, fire detection and fire extinction: the fire
  safety regime, with the FSS Code alongside.
- Chapter III — Life-saving appliances and arrangements: lifeboats, liferafts,
  lifejackets, drills — with the LSA Code.
- Chapter IV — Radiocommunications: the GMDSS distress and safety
  communications fit.
- Chapter V — Safety of navigation: applies to almost all ships; voyage
  planning, bridge equipment (AIS, ECDIS, VDR), ships' routeing, and services
  such as VTS; also the master's discretion and distress obligations.
- Chapter VI — Carriage of cargoes: general cargo safety, stowage and
  securing, grain; regulation 2 carries the Verified Gross Mass (VGM)
  requirement for packed export containers — implemented locally by circular
  CIRC-07/2024 in the Mundra demo portal.
- Chapter VII — Carriage of dangerous goods: makes the IMDG Code mandatory for
  packaged dangerous goods, with related codes for bulk chemicals and gases.
- Chapter VIII — Nuclear ships: special requirements for the small class of
  nuclear-powered ships.
- Chapter IX — Management for the safe operation of ships: makes the ISM Code
  mandatory — the safety management system, the Document of Compliance for the
  company and the Safety Management Certificate for the ship.
- Chapter X — High-speed craft: applies the HSC Code.
- Chapter XI-1 — Special measures to enhance maritime safety: enhanced
  surveys, ship identification number, continuous synopsis record.
- Chapter XI-2 — Special measures to enhance maritime security: the ISPS Code
  hook — see the ISPS overview in this library.
- Chapter XII — Additional safety measures for bulk carriers: structural
  requirements born of bulk-carrier casualties.
- Chapter XIII — Verification of compliance: makes the IMO member-state audit
  scheme (III Code) mandatory.
- Chapter XIV — Polar waters: makes the Polar Code's safety part mandatory.
- A further chapter (2024) governs ships carrying industrial personnel (IP
  Code).

## Port relevance

At a working port, SOLAS shows up as: certificates checked during PSC (Chapter
I), the VGM gate procedure (VI/2), DG declarations and IMDG segregation (VII),
ISM audits behind vessel-quality screening (IX), and the ISPS regime at the
ship–port interface (XI-2).
