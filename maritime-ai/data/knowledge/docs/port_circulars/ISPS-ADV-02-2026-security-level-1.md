## 1. Security level

1.1 Security Level 1 (MARSEC Level 1 — normal, the level at which the port
facility routinely operates) remains in force for all port facilities within
Mundra port limits. Any change of level will be promulgated by the Port
Facility Security Officer (PFSO) by broadcast, portal notice and agent message,
and takes effect immediately on promulgation.

1.2 Ships alongside shall operate their Ship Security Plans consistent with
Level 1 and shall report their current security level on the pre-arrival
security information. A Declaration of Security (DoS) is available on request
to the PFSO where a ship or the facility considers it necessary.

## 2. Access control reminders

2.1 Dock passes: every person within the restricted area shall display a valid
dock pass or biometric access card. Passes are personal; tailgating through
gates or lending of passes is a reportable security breach.

2.2 Crew shore leave: crew shore leave is routed via Gate 3 only, against the
crew list on file and the vessel's shore-leave slip. The ISPS patrol at Gate 3
logs crew out and in. Shore leave remains a facilitation obligation and is not
denied at Level 1 except for cause.

2.3 Vehicles: cargo road vehicles use the designated truck lanes at Gate 1
(CISF post). Private vehicles do not proceed beyond the Port User Complex
without a vehicle permit.

2.4 Visitors, contractors and vendors are escorted or badge-restricted to
their work area; ship chandler and repair-gang lists are lodged by the agent
before the service call.

## 3. Restricted areas and patrols

3.1 Restricted areas (berth aprons, yards, ISPS Zone B, the West Basin
approach) are marked and access-logged. Small craft shall keep clear of the
security zone around berthed vessels; unidentified small craft are reported to
Marine Control on VHF Ch 16 or Ch 12.

3.2 Unauthorised drone flight over the port is prohibited; sightings are
reported immediately to the PFSO and logged as security events.

## 4. Reporting

4.1 Lost passes, perimeter damage, tailgating attempts, suspicious packages or
persons, and any probing of access controls are reported to the PFSO and
recorded in the incident-management system as SECURITY events with the
appropriate severity.

## 5. Reference

5.1 The ISPS Code framework (security levels 1–3, roles of the CSO, SSO and
PFSO, ship and port facility security plans and assessments) is summarised in
the Sagar Drishti library: "ISPS Code — levels and roles overview".

Issued by the Port Facility Security Officer, Mundra, under the port facility
