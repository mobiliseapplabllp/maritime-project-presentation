# Port Notice PORT-N-02/2023 — Cyclone Biparjoy contingency berthing restrictions, Kutch coast (WITHDRAWN)

> Demo-world instrument of the Mundra Port Operations Portal, reproduced in the
> Sagar Drishti knowledge library. This is a fictional demo document and not a
> publication of any real port authority. (Cyclone Biparjoy itself was a real
> June 2023 weather event on the Kutch coast; the notice text is the demo
> world's own.)

Ref No: PORT-N-02/2023 · Type: NOTICE · Category: Port operations · Status: WITHDRAWN
Issued by: Harbour Master, Mundra · Issued: 12 June 2023 · Effective: 13 June 2023
Acknowledgement required: No
Tags: cyclone, contingency, weather, evacuation

Register summary (verbatim): "Precautionary berthing suspension and vessel
evacuation to sea ahead of Very Severe Cyclonic Storm Biparjoy making landfall
near Jakhau, Kutch."

## Text of the notice (as issued)

"Following IMD tracking of Very Severe Cyclonic Storm Biparjoy, all vessels
alongside shall complete cargo operations and vacate berths by 1800 hrs on
14 June 2023, proceeding to open sea or a designated safe anchorage. Port
operations will remain suspended until an all-clear survey of berths, cranes
and navigational aids is completed after landfall."

## 1. Sequence ordered by this notice (June 2023)

1.1 On issue (12 June): no fresh berthing; vessels at anchorage held pending
the storm track; agents advised masters to keep engines on notice.

1.2 By 1800 hrs, 14 June: all vessels alongside to have completed or secured
cargo operations, disconnected shore connections, and vacated berths under
pilotage, proceeding to open sea with adequate sea room or to the designated
safe anchorage as allocated by Marine Control.

1.3 During passage of the storm: port closed to all movements; tugs and craft
secured at their storm moorings; gantry cranes boomed up, twist-locked and
storm-pinned per terminal severe-weather plans; loose cargo gear and lashing
bridges cleared.

1.4 After landfall: resumption only on the Harbour Master's all-clear,
following (a) a hydrographic check sweep of the approach channel and berth
pockets by the survey launch, (b) inspection of fenders, bollards and cope
lines at every berth, (c) function checks of cranes, and (d) verification of
channel buoys and navigational aids, with any discrepancies promulgated as
local navigational warnings.

## 2. Status and withdrawal

2.1 The notice was withdrawn after the post-storm all-clear survey was
completed and normal operations resumed. It remains on the register as a
WITHDRAWN instrument and is retained in this library as the pattern for
cyclone-season contingency action.

## 3. Standing lessons carried into current procedure

- Early, time-bound evacuation to sea protects both ships and berth structures;
  ships ride out a cyclone more safely with sea room than alongside.
- The all-clear survey sequence (channel sweep → berth structures → cranes →
  aids to navigation) is now the standard resumption checklist and is
  exercised in the annual pre-monsoon drill (see Mundra SOP MP-03).
- Anchorage allocation during an evacuation follows Marine Control's storm
  plan, not the routine priority matrix.
- IMD advisories, port VHF broadcasts and agent messages run in parallel so
  that no master relies on a single channel.
